# Replacement feedback is a Lighting tab, and a case is off as a colour is

Cited as D-L89.

The keyboard flashes when text replacement changes what was typed (firmware D-F70): a lighting stage of its own, painted last, with a colour for each of three cases, a locality and accents. Ark edits it on Lighting as it edits every stage, on a tab built as Behaviour feedback's and Pointing modes' are, with nothing of its own: the stage's list beside its editor, the colour editor, **When and where** with the **Stage on** switch, **What this stage does**, and **Accents**. The format it arrives in is lighting domain version 5 under profile schema 4.5, the only ones Ark reads (D-L64).

## The names

The tab is **Replacement feedback**, the seventh and last stage, after Behaviour feedback. It is the firmware's name for the stage, it sits beside Combo feedback and Behaviour feedback as the same kind of thing, and its first word is the one a person already has for what the list holds (D-L84, D-L87). The three things it flashes for are its **cases**, named for what the keyboard did: **Replaced with text**, **Replaced by a macro** and **Replacement undone**. An accent lights for one case or for **Every replacement flash**. A person never reads "autocorrect" for any of it. The firmware's code calls the stage autocorrect feedback, so Ark's identifiers do, as D-L87 keeps them for the feature: the stage's id and accent table `autocorrect`, the record `autocorrectFeedback` with `textColor`, `macroColor` and `undoColor`, the edit `updateAutocorrectFeedback`. The words are `replacementOwners` and `replacementColourOwners` in `core/model/vocabulary.js`.

The stage beside it, which Ark called Key feedback, is now **Behaviour feedback**, at Noah's direction (2026-11-10): what it shows is the state of a key's behaviour (a tier firing, a run of taps waiting on its count), not any key, and the two feedback stages now each say what they report on. Its states are **behaviour states** and its accent for all of them is **Every behaviour state**. The spelling is Ark's, as on the Behaviours screen. As with autocorrect (D-L87), the code keeps the old name: the stage id `key`, the record `keyFeedback`, the accent table `keyBehavior` and its edits.

## A case is switched off with its colour

The keyboard stores no switch for a case: a colour with saturation and brightness both 0 paints nothing for it, and the other two still flash, the rule a layer colour has. So Ark switches a case off where it turns any colour off, with the colour editor's **Turn off**, which stores HSV(0, 0, 0); the colour then reads **Off**, the list says **off** beside the case, and **Give it a colour** brings it back. The tip and the note say what off means here (the case does not flash), since on Pointing modes, Combo feedback and Behaviour feedback the same button stores a black that is painted. A colour read from a keyboard with no saturation or brightness at another hue is off too, in the list, the board and Review; one with brightness alone at zero flashes black and says **black**, as a layer's does. An accent that takes its case's colour stays dark while the case is off, and one in a colour of its own still lights. The rule is `replacementPaints`, beside the layer's in `rules/led-light.mjs`.

## Where it paints, and how long

The localities are the five every feedback stage has, in the same words. Two of them follow "the trigger key", which here is the key that ended the word (Space, Enter, a punctuation key) or the Backspace that undid the replacement, every key of a combo; the tab says so under the field, and that the flash paints both halves, or every key, when the keyboard cannot tell which key it was. The field is the menu the other stages' Locality is.

A flash has a length of its own, **Flash length**, in milliseconds, under the Locality on this tab, as Behaviour feedback's **Flash interval** is under its Locality: the same field, its own stored setting (firmware settings scalar 31, settings domain version 12), saved as a settings section of one field and reviewed beside the stage. At first the flash lasted one Behaviour feedback flash interval (scalar 15), and the tab said so in a sentence with a link to that field, since a stored setting has one field. At Noah's direction (2026-11-10) the firmware gave the flash a length of its own, so a quick flash on keys and a longer one for a replacement, or the other way round, are set apart, and the tab has the field instead of the sentence and the link.

While Text replacement is switched off on Host settings nothing is replaced, so nothing flashes: the tab reads off and carries the banner that goes to the switch, as Behaviour feedback does while behaviours are off, and its controls stay editable (`stageHeldOffBy`).

## What the board shows

On this tab the board shows the picked case's flash where the keyboard would paint it, over everything else, with its accents, those for every case painted before those for the case. The board cannot know which key will end a word, so one key stands in: the first that sends Space, on the layer shown and then from the base up, or the first that sends Backspace for Replacement undone (`replacementTrigger`). While the locality follows a key that key is ringed, and the line under the board names it. A profile with no such key shows the flash as the keyboard paints one whose key it cannot tell. A case that is off shows no flash and the line says so. The board shows where the flash lands, held still, not the flash in time, as it shows where the auto-mouse fade lands.

## Considered options

A section of the Behaviour feedback tab: the firmware made it a stage of its own, with its own switch and accent table, and a tab per stage is what the paint order on the tabs means. A switch beside each colour: a second control for the three bytes the colour editor already owns, which would have to invent a colour when switched back on. Passthrough's words for an off case (D-L77's layer): passthrough says what is beneath shows through a layer that is on; an off case is a flash that never happens, and Off already says that. "Autocorrect feedback" as the name: the feature is Text replacement wherever it is read. Animating the flash on the board: every other stage is drawn held still, and a 200 ms blink would hide what is being edited. Ringing the last key of the word typed instead of the key that ended it: the firmware chose the ending key, and the board follows the keyboard.
