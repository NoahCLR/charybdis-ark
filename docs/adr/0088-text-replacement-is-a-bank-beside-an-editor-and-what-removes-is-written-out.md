# Text replacement is a bank beside an editor, and what removes is written out

Cited as D-L88.

The Text replacement screen is built as Macros, Custom keys and Pointing modes are: a **bank** at the left and the **editor** of the item picked beside it (`.pad.library`). It was first a table of its own, a row per replacement edited in place, with a toolbar, a panel under each chip and a card of its own for Try it. Nothing else in Ark looked or worked like it, and a person who knew Macros had to learn it from nothing. It now has no component of its own where Ark had one.

- **The bank** is a card named Replacements with how much of what the keyboard holds is used ("15 of 1,024 inputs", the ceiling an edit meets first, D-L84), a search over replacements and inputs, the items, **+ New replacement**, the list's files (**Import…** and **Export**, small, under the items where a bank folds its unused slots), and **Text replacement memory** as Macros shows Macro memory. An item is a bank tile drawn as a row, since a replacement and its inputs are text: the replacement on one line, as core calls it (a macro as "▶ name", D-L84), its inputs under it. The list is in dictionary order; the order control went, since a search finds anything faster than a second order. While searching, the bank shows what was found and nothing else, as Macros' does: **+ New replacement** comes back with the list.
- **The editor** is one card for the replacement picked: a title bar with the screen's icon, the replacement as its title, a small tag (Text or Macro) and **Delete replacement** written out in red at the right; then sections under small-capital headings, **Replacement**, **Inputs** and **Try it**. The entry picked stays open across redraws and screens, as a bank's selection does, and the first is open when none is picked: a list that has replacements always has one open, also when a search finds nothing. Text or Macro chosen and not kept yet says so under the choice, since the header and the list still have the other.
- **A replacement's macro is picked in the keycode picker**, as a custom key's shortcut is: a read-only field names the macro and **Pick…** beside it (or a click on the field) opens the picker on the macros alone (`allowedKeys`). It offers only macros that have steps, by name, and the one already picked; an empty macro would play nothing. With none, Pick… is off and the editor offers **Make one in Macros →**. A field that takes no key of the board is not offered the board. There is no menu of 128 slots anywhere. **Edit in Macros →** sits in the editor's header, as a macro's **Place on a key →** does, and goes to the macro.
- **How it works is behind info marks, not written under each section.** The Replacement, Inputs and Try it headings each carry the info mark (`ui/info-mark.mjs`), the one component the Keymap board's shortcuts mark is too, in its corner form: hovering it or reaching it by Tab shows a short list (when it replaces, capitals, Backspace, macros; how `*` is written; what the preview cannot show), a screen reader hearing it as the mark's description, and Escape shuts it. The component keeps an open list inside the screen's area: moved sideways, opened upward where there is more room, and no taller than that room, its body scrolling. The figures in a list (128 bytes, 32 bytes, four letters, the starter list's size) are the keyboard's, as the list publishes them. The sections themselves carry only their controls and their warnings.
- **Inputs are chips.** **+ Input** becomes a field that adds one, or several with spaces between, and is ready for the next at once: what was entered waits its turn to be checked and posted (D-L84), and what core refuses comes back into the field with the reason under it. A chip opens in place to be written anew, with **Remove** beside its field. Moving an input to another replacement went, from the screen and from the edit: it was a second field in every chip for something Remove and + Input do in two steps.
- **New replacement** opens an editor with nothing in the list behind it, its text focused, as New macro opens a slot. A replacement always has an input (D-L84), so it joins the list with its first one, kept when Enter is pressed in it or the editor is left from it for another replacement or screen; what was typed for it stays with it while it waits in the bank. One the list already has, which core's check says, is offered to be opened instead, and the checks refuse a duplicate as before.
- **With no replacements** the editor's place shows the empty pattern a macro with no steps has ("Your replacements start here"), with the starter list as its one primary action.
- **What heads the screen** (the switch's banner, and what the host layout cannot type, which core says whole) runs across both panes (`.library-head`), each the same callout.
- **A file's summary is a sheet over the screen**, as a backup's comparison is, with the question's foot every dialog has (`dialogFoot`): Cancel, Add to my list, then Replace my list, written in red and last, since it deletes every replacement the list has and a destructive choice that is not the primary goes last. While the system's chooser is asking for a file nothing is drawn behind it. Export's formats, named by the vocabulary, are a menu drawn as the rail's menu is and driven by the same keys (`lib/menu-keys.mjs`).

## What removes is written out

A control that removes, deletes, clears or discards something says so in words, in the red of every destructive action: Clear macro, Remove behaviour, Delete replacement, Remove, Discard, Replace my list. A replacement is **deleted** and an input **removed**, in every button, tip, history step and summary. A control with nothing to remove is off, as Try it's Clear is with an empty box. It is never a bare × or an icon alone. A × reads as "close" as easily as "delete", says nothing of what goes, and is a small target for something that loses work. This holds for the whole of text replacement and is the rule for new work: an input chip carries no ×, and opens to a field with **Remove** beside it. `browser-tests/autocorrect.spec.js` holds the screen to it.

Older controls that clear a field with a small × (a pointing mode's direction key, the chips of the picker's selection) are not changed here; they are the exception to bring in line, not a pattern to follow.

## The rest of the feature, matched to its neighbours

Every surface text replacement touches is the one its neighbours use, not one of its own:

- **The Host card** is a settings row and nothing else, as the switches of combos and behaviours are. Its link to the list (**Edit replacements →**, the only such link on any card) went; its one line says what off does and where the list is, as the combo switch says "in Keymap → Combos".
- **The `AC_*` keys** are in the picker's Switch keys with those for combos, behaviours and auto-mouse, worded alike (Text replacement On, Text replacement Off, Toggle Text replacement), and like them have no help of their own: a tile's tip is its keycode. What a switch key does is said once for all of them, in the guide.
- **Review** lists an entry as it lists a macro or a custom key: its name, then a line for each part that changed (Replacement, Input).
- **Memory** on the Keyboard screen counts **Text replacement inputs** among what is in use and shows **Text replacement memory** after Macro memory.
- **Checks and warnings** are the notes and callouts of every screen (`.note.warn`, `.unavailable`), and Review's findings are profile checks like any other.

## Considered options

Keeping the table and restyling it: it would still have been the only list in Ark edited in place, with a toolbar no other screen has. Tiles in a grid, as Macros has: a tile holds thirteen characters, and a replacement is a sentence with words under it. A × on each chip with the chip's text opening the field: two targets in one chip, one of them a bare ×. An **Add** button for a new replacement: nothing else in an editor is saved by a button, and the first input is the moment it becomes an entry. Giving every switch key the help the `AC_*` keys had: a change to three other features to keep one sentence, which the guide already says.
