# Key legends are set from one scale

Cited as D-L78.

The board sets every key's legends from one small scale instead of sizing each to its own width, so the board reads as one calm surface on every layer and every screen. Sized to its width, each name took its own size: the demo's layers drew fourteen legend sizes, "Left Ctrl" and "Left Shift" side by side at two of them, and Lighting shrank a hold again to clear the LED's number. On the scale, a single character, as a keycap prints a letter, digit or symbol, is drawn large (12); every name, however short (Esc, Tab, Left Shift, a macro's), at one word size (9); a dual-role key's hold under its line at one size of its own (6.6), condensed into the row when too long. Legends run at the face's own spacing, untightened. A name of two or more words always takes two lines, so whether a key wraps never depends on how wide its words happen to be, and "Left Thumb" and "Right Thumb" read alike: it breaks at a " · " when it has one, dropping the dot, else between words where the longer line comes out shortest, never starting a line with a number or leaving one on its own ("Macro 0" stays whole). A tap over a line keeps one line. A line too wide for the cap is condensed, keeping its height, by up to 14% before the legend steps down to one smaller size (7.6), and past that is condensed into the row; a word is never cut. Whether a line fits is measured in the page's own face, which is the system's and differs between computers. Where the board shows LEDs (Lighting), each key carries its LED's number on a small label pinned over its top-left corner and the trackball in its middle, apart from the legends, which never move or shrink for it: a key reads the same on every screen. The rule is `keyLegends` in `webview/view/geometry.mjs`; the contract is in [Shared controls](../spec/interface-contracts.md#shared-controls).

## Considered options

Fitting each legend to its row, the rule this replaces: no two names share a size, which is the noise this removes. Two sizes, wrapping only a name that does not fit on one line: whether a key wraps then turns on a few units of width, so "Right Thumb" wrapped beside a one-line "Left Thumb". Wrapping a Left key whenever its Right sibling would: it pairs the sides but leaves the same edge between any two neighbours ("Right Option" on one line beside a wrapped "Left Command"). Short names on the caps ("L Shift", "Bksp"): calmer still, but a second name for every key to learn beside the one the hover card, the picker and Review use. One size for every legend: letters lose their presence. Words in small spaced capitals: distinctive, but hard to read at a laptop's width. A typeface shipped with Ark (Inter, Geist, Aileron, Instrument Sans): looked at beside the system face, which reads best on a Mac and is Segoe UI on Windows; the system face stays.
