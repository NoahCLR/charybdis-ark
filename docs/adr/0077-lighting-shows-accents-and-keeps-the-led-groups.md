# Lighting shows accents and keeps the LED groups

Cited as D-L77.

A person colours single LEDs as **accents**: LEDs that light in a colour of their own while a layer is on, a pointing mode runs, a combo's inputs are held or a key shows a feedback state. Each stage's tab on Lighting lists the accents of the owner it has picked (the layer shown, the pointing mode, the feedback state), with those lighting for all of them tagged among them, and adds, changes and removes them there; whether an accent lights for one owner or all is its own choice, not a view to switch to. All accents lists every one. The keyboard stores an accent as a row in its stage's table that names a reusable group of LEDs (firmware [RGB domain](../../upstream/firmware/docs/spec/rgb-domain-v1.md#group-rows)), but a group carries no name, its id is its place in a sorted list, and only the LEDs reach the keyboard, so Ark keeps the groups and never shows one: an accent names its LEDs, an accent lighting LEDs another already lights shares its group, and every accent edit drops a group no accent lights, renumbering the rest. The keyboard's two limits read as accents (32) and LED sets (16). An accent is changed where it stands in its table (`updateRgbLedGroup`), since a later row repaints an earlier one where they overlap. LEDs are named by the index the keyboard numbers them with, "LEDs 24–28, 53–55" (`ledsCalled` in `core/model/vocabulary.js`, published on each accent as `leds`), and the board on Lighting numbers every key and the trackball by its LED, so the words and the board agree on every layer, mapped or not. Picking an accent's LEDs happens on the lit board itself, by press or drag, which shows the LEDs in the accent's colour before it is kept. The interface's rules are in [Lighting preview](../spec/interface-contracts.md#lighting-preview).

## Considered options

A tab of its own where a person picks a table, an owner and a saved or inline group, then a colour: the screen this replaces, which exposed four storage words for one intent, renumbered groups under saved choices, left unused groups holding slots and could only remove a row, never change one. Painting keys with colour brushes per moment, compiled into a row per colour: quicker to use, but stored rows that overlap do not read back as one picture and every colour costs a group. Named regions of keys (thumbs, arrows) against the moments they light: names never reach the keyboard, so they would read back unnamed on another computer. Naming LEDs by the legends of their keys: a layer's keys change and an unmapped one has none, so an index is the one name that holds everywhere.
